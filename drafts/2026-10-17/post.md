## Headnote

En papillote is one of the more honest French techniques. You seal the fish with whatever aromatics you like, the steam does the work, and the parcel opens at the table in a small cloud that announces itself before anyone takes a bite. I started using it for spiced fish because the paper traps volatile aromatics — the Kashmiri chilli, the ginger — that would otherwise cook off or harden in a pan. The coconut oil adds a fat that can handle the spice without competing with the delicate cod. This is not a fish curry in the subcontinental sense: no sauce, no long simmer. It is what happens when you apply Pakistani spice logic to a French method. The result is its own thing.

## Ingredients

### Fish and marinade
- 4 cod fillets, skin-off, 160–180 g (5.5–6.5 oz) each
- 2 tbsp coconut oil, melted
- 1 tsp Kashmiri chilli powder
- ½ tsp ground cumin
- ½ tsp ground coriander
- ¼ tsp turmeric
- 1 tsp fine sea salt
- 1 tbsp fresh lemon juice

### Aromatics and base
- 20 g (¾ oz) fresh ginger, peeled and cut into thin matchsticks
- 3 garlic cloves, thinly sliced
- 2 medium tomatoes, finely diced (about 180 g / 6.5 oz total)
- 1 small red onion, halved and thinly sliced
- 8 fresh curry leaves (or 12 dried)
- 1 green chilli, slit lengthways (seeds in for heat, out for mild)

### To finish
- 4 tsp coconut oil (one per parcel)
- 4 thin lemon slices
- 2 tbsp fresh coriander leaves, roughly torn
- Flaky sea salt, to finish

## Ingredient Notes

**Kashmiri chilli powder:** This is not interchangeable with generic red chilli powder. Kashmiri chilli is low heat, high colour — it gives a deep brick-red without turning the fish aggressive. Find it at any South Asian grocery. If unavailable, use 1 tsp sweet smoked paprika plus a pinch of cayenne.

**Coconut oil:** Use unrefined (virgin) coconut oil for a mild tropical note that works well with the spice profile. Refined coconut oil is neutral and will also work. Do not substitute vegetable oil; you will lose a flavour layer that holds the dish together.

**Curry leaves:** Fresh is better. If your South Asian grocery has a freezer section, they are almost always there. Dried work but should be used in larger quantity and crumbled slightly before use to release the oils.

**Cod:** This recipe suits any firm white fish that flakes cleanly. Haddock, pollock, or sea bass all work. Avoid thin fillets under 130 g (4.5 oz) — they will overcook before the aromatics bloom.

## Method

1. **Preheat the oven to 200°C (400°F / Gas 6).** You want the oven fully up to temperature before the parcels go in; a sluggish oven extends the cooking time unevenly.

2. **Make the spice paste.** In a small bowl, combine the melted coconut oil, Kashmiri chilli powder, cumin, coriander, turmeric, salt, and lemon juice. Stir until it forms a loose, even paste.

3. **Coat the fish.** Pat the cod fillets dry with kitchen paper. Dry fish browns and absorbs spice better than wet fish. Coat each fillet all over with the spice paste and leave for 10 minutes at room temperature while you prepare the parcels.

4. **Cut four sheets of parchment paper**, each approximately 40 × 30 cm (16 × 12 inches). You can also use foil if you prefer a more robust seal, but parchment gives a better visual presentation when you open at the table.

5. **Build each parcel.** Lay a parchment sheet on a flat surface. In the centre, place a small mound of red onion, then a quarter of the diced tomato, a few slices of garlic, a quarter of the ginger matchsticks, two curry leaves, and a quarter of the green chilli. This base lifts the fish slightly off the paper so steam can circulate underneath.

6. **Place a cod fillet on top of the aromatics.** Add one lemon slice on top of the fillet, drizzle over 1 tsp of coconut oil, and scatter a few torn coriander leaves.

7. **Seal the parcel.** Bring the long edges of the parchment up over the fish and fold them together in tight, overlapping pleats — three or four folds — working from the centre outward. Twist or fold the short ends closed firmly. The seal needs to hold steam; a loose fold will let it escape and the fish will dry rather than steam.

8. **Bake for 18–20 minutes.** For fillets around 160–170 g (5.5–6 oz), 18 minutes gives a just-set, very moist fillet. At 180 g (6.5 oz), give it the full 20 minutes. Do not open the parcels mid-cook to check; you release the steam and interrupt the process.

9. **Rest for 2 minutes** on a board before serving. The parcel continues to steam after leaving the oven; this rest completes the carry-over cooking and lets the internal temperature stabilise.

10. **Open at the table.** Cut or tear each parcel open carefully — the steam is genuinely hot. Finish with a pinch of flaky sea salt over the fish and a few fresh coriander leaves if you kept any back.

## Variations

**Cheaper cut:** Replace cod with pollock, which is significantly less expensive, sustainably sourced in most European markets, and handles steam well. Cook time stays the same.

**More Kashmiri character:** Add ¼ tsp hing to the spice paste and scatter four or five saffron threads over the fish before sealing. This moves the dish toward a Kashmiri wazwan sensibility — warm, fragrant, slightly floral.

**Vegetarian version:** Replace the fish with a 200 g (7 oz) block of firm paneer per parcel, sliced 1 cm (½ inch) thick. Increase bake time to 22 minutes. The paneer will absorb the aromatics and colour deeply from the Kashmiri chilli.

**Citrus variation:** Swap the lemon slice for a thin round of kaffir lime and replace one tablespoon of the diced tomato with finely diced raw mango. This pulls the dish toward a Southeast Asian register while keeping the Pakistani spice base.

## Storage and Reheating

Cod curry en papillote is at its best immediately out of the oven. If you need to store it, transfer the cooked fish (removed from the paper) to an airtight container and refrigerate within 45 minutes of cooking. It keeps for up to 2 days.

To reheat: place the fish in a small ovenproof dish, add 2 tbsp of water, cover tightly with foil, and warm at 160°C (320°F) for 10–12 minutes. Do not microwave; the texture of reheated fish degrades significantly and the spice notes flatten.

Do not freeze. Cod reheated from frozen loses its flake structure entirely.

## FAQ

### What does cod curry en papillote taste like compared to a traditional curry?
Cod curry en papillote has a cleaner, more delicate flavour than a sauced curry — the Kashmiri chilli and ginger perfume the fish without coating it. The steam cooking preserves the fresh, aromatic quality of the spices rather than cooking them into a deep, long-reduced base.

### Can I use frozen cod for this baked cod curry?
Yes, but thaw the fillets completely in the refrigerator overnight and pat them very dry before marinating. Excess water from frozen fish will pool in the parcel and dilute the spice paste, producing a steamed rather than spice-infused result.

### How do I know the fish is cooked inside the sealed parcel?
A 160–180 g (5.5–6.5 oz) cod fillet at 200°C (400°F) in a well-sealed parchment parcel is fully cooked in 18–20 minutes. If you want to verify, open one parcel after 18 minutes and press the thickest part with a finger — fully cooked cod yields and flakes apart without resistance or translucency.

### Can I prepare the parcels in advance?
You can assemble the sealed parcels up to 4 hours ahead and refrigerate them. Take them out 15 minutes before baking to bring them closer to room temperature, then bake as directed. Do not marinate the fish in the spice paste for longer than 1 hour, as the lemon juice will begin to denature the proteins and the texture becomes chalky.

### Is this fish en papillote curry very spicy?
Kashmiri chilli is a low-heat chilli used primarily for colour and a mild warmth. As written, the dish is mild-to-medium. The green chilli with seeds adds more heat; remove the seeds or omit it entirely for a mild result.

### What do I serve with this?
Steamed basmati, a simple cucumber raita, or warm flatbread all work. The parcels release some concentrated juices that function as a light sauce — you want something to absorb them. A green salad alongside is also a clean choice if you want to keep the meal light.
