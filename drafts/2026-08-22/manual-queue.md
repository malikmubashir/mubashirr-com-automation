The dish looks interesting and tastes confused.\n\nRatatouille masala started as a question about technique, not flavour. The French approach to ratatouille is about sequence and patience: each vegetable given time, layers built slowly, nothing rushed. The South Asian approach to spice is also about sequence: whole spices into hot oil first, aromatics second, ground spices third, each stage doing something the next stage cannot undo.\n\nThose two logics are compatible. More than compatible — they reinforce each other.\n\nThe result is a dish where courgette, aubergine and pepper cook in the Provençal manner, but the foundation is a cumin and mustard seed temper with turmeric pressed into the base. Neither tradition is decorative. Both are structural.\n\nI find this a useful way to think about cross-cultural work more broadly. The question is rarely which tradition wins. The question is whether the underlying logics can hold each other up. When they can, the outcome is not a hybrid. It is something that could not have existed without both."
