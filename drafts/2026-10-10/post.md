## Headnote

A quiche Lorraine is, at its core, a custard vehicle. The lardons are there for fat and smoke; the Gruyère for salt and depth. Once you understand that, you start seeing what else can carry the same logic. Chicken tikka — charred, spiced, already fully developed in flavour — does the job with more character than bacon ever managed. The leeks slow-cook until they are almost jammy, which keeps the custard from reading as dry. Whole-wheat pastry adds a nuttiness that shortcrust made from plain flour lacks, and it holds up better against the moisture of the filling. I use a cumin-and-coriander short-crust here because it ties the two cuisines without making a fuss about doing so. This is not quiche lorraine. It is what quiche lorraine teaches you to make when you cook Pakistani and live in France.

## Ingredients

### Whole-wheat cumin pastry
- 150 g (1¼ cups) whole-wheat flour
- 75 g (⅔ cup) plain flour, plus extra for dusting
- ½ tsp fine sea salt
- ½ tsp ground cumin
- ¼ tsp ground coriander
- 130 g (9 tbsp) cold unsalted butter, cut into 1 cm cubes
- 3–4 tbsp ice-cold water

### Tikka chicken
- 400 g (14 oz) boneless chicken thighs, skinless
- 3 tbsp plain full-fat yoghurt
- 1 tbsp neutral oil
- 1 tsp Kashmiri red chilli powder
- 1 tsp ground cumin
- ½ tsp ground coriander
- ½ tsp garam masala
- ½ tsp ground turmeric
- ½ tsp fine sea salt
- 1 tsp ginger-garlic paste
- 1 tsp lemon juice

### Filling
- 2 medium leeks (approx. 250 g / 9 oz trimmed weight), thinly sliced
- 1 tbsp unsalted butter
- 1 tbsp neutral oil
- ¼ tsp fine sea salt
- 100 g (3½ oz) Gruyère, coarsely grated

### Custard
- 3 large eggs
- 1 large egg yolk
- 250 ml (1 cup) double cream
- 100 ml (7 tbsp) whole milk
- ¼ tsp fine sea salt
- ¼ tsp white pepper
- A small pinch of hing
- 2 tbsp finely chopped flat-leaf parsley

## Ingredient notes

**Kashmiri chilli powder** gives colour more than heat. If you can only find standard Degi mirch, use it; the flavour is nearly identical. Don't substitute cayenne or regular chilli flakes — the colour and smoke profile are wrong.

**Hing** (asafoetida). A pinch in the custard reads as a low bass note, not as a detectable flavour. It mimics the allium depth that lardons would provide in a classic lorraine. Find it at any Pakistani or Indian grocer; the compounded yellow powder (flour-cut) is what most kitchens use.

**Gruyère.** French Gruyère from Comté or Jura has a nuttier, more complex flavour than Swiss. Either works. Emmental is a reasonable substitute but milder. Comté is even better if your shop carries it.

**Whole-wheat flour.** This pastry works with atta (Pakistani chapati flour), which is slightly finer than British wholemeal. If using atta, reduce the water by 1 tablespoon — it absorbs more readily.

**Double cream.** In France this is crème entière (30%+ fat). Do not reduce the fat content; the custard needs it to set with a silky texture rather than a rubbery one.

## Method

1. **Marinate the chicken.** Combine all tikka marinade ingredients in a bowl. Add the chicken thighs, coat thoroughly, cover and refrigerate for at least 1 hour, or overnight.

2. **Make the pastry.** Combine both flours, salt, cumin and coriander in a wide bowl. Add the cold butter and rub quickly between your fingertips until the mixture resembles coarse breadcrumbs with some flat, pea-sized pieces remaining — those pockets of butter are what create flakiness. Add ice-cold water one tablespoon at a time, pressing with a fork until the dough just comes together. Do not knead. Flatten into a disc, wrap and refrigerate for 30 minutes.

3. **Grill the chicken.** Heat a grill pan or cast-iron skillet over high heat until smoking. Cook the marinated thighs for 5–6 minutes per side, pressing lightly so you get char marks. The internal temperature should reach 75°C (167°F). Rest for 5 minutes, then cut into 2 cm cubes. Set aside. (Browning the chicken separately before it goes into the quiche means it stays distinct in texture rather than steaming into the custard.)

4. **Cook the leeks.** Melt the butter with the oil in a medium saucepan over medium-low heat. Add the sliced leeks and salt. Stir, then cook uncovered for 18–20 minutes, stirring occasionally, until the leeks are completely soft, pale golden, and smell sweet. There should be no raw allium sharpness remaining. Spread onto a plate to cool.

5. **Blind-bake the shell.** Preheat the oven to 190°C (375°F). Roll the pastry on a lightly floured surface to about 3 mm thick and line a 23 cm (9-inch) loose-bottomed tart tin, pressing into the corners without stretching. Trim the overhang to 1 cm above the tin rim (it will shrink). Prick the base all over with a fork. Line with baking paper, fill with baking beans or dried chickpeas, and blind-bake for 15 minutes. Remove the beans and paper, and bake a further 8 minutes until the base is dry and just beginning to colour. Reduce the oven to 170°C (340°F).

6. **Make the custard.** Whisk together the eggs, egg yolk, cream, milk, salt, white pepper, and hing until smooth. Stir in the parsley. To avoid a scrambled texture, do not pour cold custard into a hot shell; instead, let the shell cool for 5 minutes off the oven before assembly.

7. **Assemble.** Scatter the cooked leeks evenly over the pastry base. Distribute the tikka chicken cubes over the leeks. Sprinkle three-quarters of the Gruyère over the top. Carefully pour the custard mixture over everything, filling to within 3 mm of the pastry rim. Scatter the remaining Gruyère on the surface.

8. **Bake.** Slide the tart tin onto a baking sheet (to catch any drips) and bake at 170°C (340°F) for 32–36 minutes, until the custard is just set at the edges but still has a very slight wobble at the very centre — the residual heat will finish it. An internal temperature of 72°C (162°F) at the centre confirms it is done. Remove and cool for at least 15 minutes before unmoulding. Cutting too early collapses the custard.

## Variations

**Cheaper cut:** Replace chicken thighs with drumstick meat (skin off, bones removed after cooking). The texture is slightly more fibrous but takes the tikka marinade just as well.

**Vegetarian version:** Replace chicken with 300 g (10½ oz) paneer, cut into 2 cm cubes, marinated in the same tikka marinade and griddled until charred. Add 1 teaspoon of smoked paprika to the custard to compensate for the loss of chicken depth.

**Different vegetable:** Swap leeks for 200 g (7 oz) baby spinach wilted with a clove of garlic and squeezed very dry. The quiche reads greener and slightly more mineral, which pairs well with the tikka spicing.

**Different cheese:** Comté aged 12 months replaces Gruyère for a nuttier, more pronouncedly French character. Mild cheddar works but flattens the profile; use it only if nothing else is available.

## Storage and reheating

Cool the quiche completely to room temperature before storing. Wrap the tin (or transfer slices to an airtight container) and refrigerate for up to 3 days. The pastry softens slightly on day two but the flavour improves.

To reheat, place slices on a baking sheet and warm in a 160°C (320°F) oven for 12 minutes. Microwave reheating works but the pastry goes limp; acceptable for lunch, not for guests.

Freeze individual slices wrapped tightly in cling film then foil for up to 6 weeks. Reheat from frozen at 160°C (320°F) for 20 minutes.

## FAQ

### Can I make the chicken tikka quiche ahead of time?
Yes. Bake the quiche fully, cool completely, and refrigerate up to 2 days ahead. Reheat in a 160°C oven for 12 minutes before serving; the custard texture is nearly indistinguishable from freshly baked.

### Does this chicken tikka quiche work without blind-baking the shell?
Skipping blind-baking will give you a soggy base because the wet custard soaks into raw pastry before it sets. Blind-baking the shell first is not optional in this recipe — it takes 23 minutes and determines whether the base is crisp or soft.

### What makes this a halal quiche recipe?
This recipe contains no pork, no alcohol, and no non-halal gelatin. Ensure your butter and cream are standard dairy, and that the chicken is halal-certified. Every other ingredient is inherently permissible.

### Why whole-wheat flour in the pastry?
Whole-wheat flour adds a slightly bitter, nutty note that complements the Kashmiri chilli and cumin in the filling. It also produces a more robust shell that does not turn translucent and soft from the custard's moisture.

### My custard puffed up and cracked. What went wrong?
The oven was too hot or the quiche baked too long. At 170°C (340°F) the custard should barely tremble at the centre when you pull it. Cracking means it went past set into soufflé territory; still edible, but the texture will be slightly grainy rather than silky.

### Can I use chicken breast instead of thighs?
You can, but the tikka cubes will be drier and less forgiving if the oven runs even slightly hot. Thighs stay moist and pull apart slightly in the custard in a way that reads as intentional. If using breast, reduce the grill time to 4 minutes per side and do not cut the cubes smaller than 2 cm.

### Is the hing essential?
No, but it contributes a low-frequency savouriness that is otherwise absent now that bacon is not in the picture. Leave it out if you do not have it; the quiche is still complete. Add a small extra pinch of white pepper to compensate.
