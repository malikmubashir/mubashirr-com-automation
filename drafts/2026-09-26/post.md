## Headnote

Merguez biryani started as a practical problem. Good lamb in our part of France is easy to find; good seekh kebab is not. Merguez, on the other hand, is sold at every halal butcher in the quartier, already spiced with harissa, cumin, and fennel, and it holds its shape when you slice it. I started treating it the way I would treat kebabs in a Pakistani layered rice — browning the coins hard so the casing crisps, then building dum on top. The result is not Pakistani biryani and it is not French. It is what you make when you have lived long enough in both places that the categories stop mattering.

The key technical insight: merguez releases a red-orange fat that is already seasoned. You cook the onions in it. You toast the rice in it. Nothing is wasted.

## Ingredients

### Rice
- 350 g (1¾ cups) aged basmati rice
- 1.2 litres (5 cups) water, for parboiling
- 2 tsp fine sea salt
- 1 bay leaf
- 4 green cardamom pods, lightly cracked
- 1 black cardamom pod

### Merguez and Onion Layer
- 500 g (about 1 lb 2 oz) halal merguez sausages
- 3 large onions (about 500 g / 1 lb 2 oz), halved and thinly sliced
- 3 tbsp neutral oil (sunflower or rapeseed)
- 1 tsp cumin seeds
- 1 tsp coriander powder
- ½ tsp smoked paprika
- ¼ tsp ground cinnamon
- ¼ tsp hing
- 2 medium tomatoes (about 200 g / 7 oz), roughly chopped
- 1 tbsp tomato paste
- 1 tsp fine sea salt
- Juice of ½ lemon

### For the Dum
- 3 tbsp full-fat plain yoghurt, whisked
- Small handful of fresh mint leaves
- Small handful of fresh coriander leaves
- 2 tbsp warm milk with a large pinch of saffron, steeped 10 min
- 1 tbsp ghee or unsalted butter
- Dough or a tight-fitting lid plus a heavy weight for sealing

## Ingredient Notes

**Merguez:** Buy halal merguez from a North African butcher or a halal supermarket. The sausage should be fresh, not pre-cooked. Merguez varies in heat level; taste a small piece raw and adjust the smoked paprika in your masala accordingly. If yours is very hot, drop the paprika to ¼ tsp.

**Hing:** A pinch of hing (asafoetida) goes into the onion base. It does something quiet and necessary to the overall depth. Don't skip it.

**Saffron:** Steep the threads in warm (not boiling) milk for at least ten minutes. Boiling kills the volatile aromatics. The milk carries colour and fragrance through the top layer of rice.

**Aged basmati:** Aged basmati is drier, starchier on the outside, and more resistant to becoming mushy during parboil. It is sold in 5 kg and 10 kg sacks at South Asian grocers and is worth the buy.

## Method

1. **Soak the rice.** Rinse the basmati in cold water three times until the water runs almost clear. Soak in fresh cold water for 30 minutes. Drain.

2. **Parboil the rice.** Bring 1.2 litres of water to a rolling boil with the salt, bay leaf, and cardamoms. Add the drained rice. Cook uncovered on high heat for exactly 6 minutes, stirring once at the start to prevent sticking. The rice should be about 70% cooked — firm at the centre, softened at the edges. Drain immediately and spread on a wide tray to stop the cooking.

3. **Brown the merguez.** Slice the sausages into 3 cm (1¼ inch) coins on a slight diagonal. Heat 1 tbsp of oil in a wide, heavy-bottomed pot (ideally a Dutch oven) over high heat. Sear the merguez coins in a single layer, without moving them, for 90 seconds per side until the casing is charred at the edges and the cut face is deeply browned. Work in batches; crowding steams instead of sears. Remove with a slotted spoon and set aside. Leave the orange fat in the pot.

4. **Caramelise the onions.** Add the remaining 2 tbsp oil to the merguez fat. Add the sliced onions and the cumin seeds. Cook over medium-high heat, stirring every two to three minutes, for 22 to 25 minutes. You are looking for deep mahogany — not amber, not pale gold. This is the non-negotiable flavour base. If the pan threatens to scorch, add a splash of water and scrape the fond.

5. **Build the masala.** Push the onions to the side. Add the hing to the bare centre of the pan, let it sizzle for 15 seconds, then stir in the coriander powder, smoked paprika, and cinnamon. Add the chopped tomatoes and tomato paste. Cook, stirring, until the tomatoes collapse and the oil separates from the masala — about 8 minutes. Season with 1 tsp salt and the lemon juice. Return the sausage coins to the pot and mix gently. Take off the heat.

6. **Layer the biryani.** Spread the masala-sausage mix in an even layer across the bottom of the pot. Drop spoonfuls of whisked yoghurt over it in five or six places. Scatter half the mint and coriander. Lay the parboiled rice over the top in a mound, higher in the centre (this allows the steam to circulate). Drizzle the saffron milk in three or four passes over the rice surface. Dot with ghee. Scatter the remaining herbs.

7. **Seal and cook on dum.** Cover the pot tightly. If you have a lid that fits well, place a folded damp kitchen towel between the lid and the pot, then weigh the lid down. Alternatively, roll a long rope of dough (flour and water), press it around the rim, and press the lid into it. Place the pot on a tawa or a heat diffuser over the lowest possible flame. Cook for 20 minutes. Do not open the lid during this time. The rice steams and the bottom layer caramelises gently.

8. **Rest and serve.** Turn off the heat and let the pot rest, still sealed, for 5 minutes. Open at the table. Use a wide spoon or spatula to lift from the bottom, serving both the saffron-tinted top rice and the bottom layer together so each plate gets both colours and the sausage coins.

## Variations

**Cheaper cut, same flavour:** Replace half the merguez with cooked chickpeas (drained, from a tin). Brown the chickpeas in the same fat after removing the sausage — they pick up the spiced oil beautifully.

**Different vegetable:** Add 200 g (7 oz) of baby spinach to the masala just before layering. It wilts into the sausage mix and adds something green to an otherwise red-orange dish.

**Whole lamb sausage:** Instead of slicing, leave four to six sausages whole, brown them on all sides, and lay them over the masala layer before topping with rice. They cook further on dum and slice at the table — more dramatic, slightly juicier inside.

**Milder version for children:** Use a mild merguez (some brands are labelled *doux*) and reduce the smoked paprika to ¼ tsp. The yoghurt layer also moderates heat.

## Storage and Reheating

Transfer leftovers to an airtight container within two hours of cooking. Refrigerate for up to three days. Do not leave biryani at room temperature; the rice sits in a spiced fat that goes rancid quickly if warmed and cooled repeatedly.

To reheat: place the portion in a wide, covered pan with 2 tbsp of water over low heat for 8 to 10 minutes, stirring once halfway. Alternatively, microwave in a covered bowl with a splash of water on medium power (not high — high turns the rice gummy) for 2 to 3 minutes.

Do not freeze. The sausage texture degrades significantly after freezing and thawing.

## FAQ

### What is merguez biryani?
Merguez biryani is a layered rice dish that combines Pakistani dum-cooking technique with North African halal lamb sausage (merguez). Spiced rice is steamed over a base of caramelised onions and browned merguez coins sealed in a covered pot.

### Can I use any merguez for this recipe?
Yes, as long as it is halal-certified and fresh rather than pre-cooked. Check the spice level of your brand first; heat levels vary considerably between butchers, and very hot merguez will make the biryani uncomfortably fiery without adjusting the other spices.

### Why do you parboil the rice to only 70% before the dum?
Biryani rice finishes cooking during the sealed dum stage. If you fully cook the rice before layering, it turns soft and pasty under the steam. 70% done means each grain still has a firm core that softens to the right texture during those final 20 minutes.

### How do I know when the onions are ready?
The onions should be deep mahogany brown — considerably darker than golden. At this colour, most of the water has evaporated and the natural sugars have caramelised, which is what gives the biryani its depth. This takes a genuine 22 to 25 minutes at medium-high heat; do not rush it.

### Can I make this without a tawa or heat diffuser?
You can use the thickest-bottomed pan you own. If your hob runs hot, keep the flame at its absolute lowest and check after 15 minutes by lifting the lid briefly. If the bottom smells of scorching rather than caramelisation, move the pot to a smaller burner or off heat entirely for the last five minutes.

### Is this the same as a lamb sausage biryani from Pakistani restaurants?
No. Pakistani restaurant biryanis typically use minced or bone-in lamb. This is a different construction: the merguez provides pre-seasoned fat that flavours the entire dish, and the sausage slices hold their texture through the dum rather than breaking apart. The result shares the format of biryani but has its own character.

### What do I serve alongside merguez biryani?
A simple raita (whisked yoghurt with grated cucumber, salt, and dried mint) is enough. The dish already carries substantial spice and fat; you want something cooling alongside it, not something that competes.
