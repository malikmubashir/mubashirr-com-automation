## Goat Cheese Stuffed Aubergine with Walnuts

Baby aubergines have a particular meatiness when roasted whole that large ones never quite achieve. The skin holds everything together; the flesh collapses into something almost custard-like. I started thinking about this combination after noticing that the spice profile of a good baingan bharta — cumin, coriander, a little heat — is not so different from what French cooks do with herbed chevre: both want an earthy, slightly acidic centre against something rich and smoky. The walnut bridges the two. It provides fat, texture, and a faint bitterness that stops the whole thing from becoming sweet. The chevre melts into the cavity rather than sitting on top of it, which matters. This is not a fusion experiment. It is a logical dish.

## Ingredients

### Aubergines
- 8 baby aubergines (about 80–90 g / 3 oz each)
- 3 tbsp olive oil
- ½ tsp fine sea salt

### Walnut and Spice Filling
- 100 g (3½ oz) walnut halves, lightly toasted
- 1 small red onion (about 80 g / 3 oz), finely minced
- 2 garlic cloves, minced
- 1 green chilli, finely chopped (seeds in or out, your call)
- 1 tsp ground cumin
- ½ tsp ground coriander
- ¼ tsp smoked paprika
- ¼ tsp ground black pepper
- 1 tbsp lemon juice
- 1 tbsp olive oil
- 3 tbsp fresh coriander leaves, roughly chopped
- ½ tsp fine sea salt

### Chevre Layer
- 150 g (5½ oz) soft chevre (fresh French goat cheese), at room temperature
- 1 tbsp good olive oil
- Pinch of dried thyme
- Black pepper to taste

### To Finish
- Extra fresh coriander
- A few walnut pieces
- A drizzle of pomegranate molasses (optional but strongly recommended)

## Ingredient Notes

**Baby aubergines:** Look for ones that are uniformly small and firm with no soft spots. South Asian grocery stores carry them reliably. If you can only find medium aubergines, halve them lengthways instead of slitting them — cooking time increases by about 10 minutes.

**Chevre:** Use a soft, fresh chevre with no rind — the type sold in logs or tubs. Aged or rinded chevre will not melt into the cavity the same way. The brand matters less than the freshness; if it smells sharp and acidic, it is working.

**Walnuts:** Toast them dry in a pan over medium heat for 3–4 minutes until fragrant before crushing. Untoasted walnuts taste flat here. Crush them with the flat of a knife or pulse twice in a food processor — you want rubble, not powder.

**Pomegranate molasses:** This is the bridge between the two cuisines. The tartness cuts the richness of the chevre; the sweetness complements the smoked paprika. Find it in any Middle Eastern or Pakistani grocery store.

## Method

1. **Preheat the oven to 200°C / 400°F (fan 180°C).** Line a baking tray with parchment.

2. **Prepare the aubergines.** Without cutting all the way through, make two perpendicular slits in each aubergine from the stem end downward, leaving the base intact so the aubergine stays in one piece. You are creating four quarters that open like a flower when pressed. Rub olive oil into the slits and over the skin. Season with salt.

3. **First roast.** Place the aubergines on the baking tray and roast for 18–20 minutes until the skins are wrinkled and the flesh inside is beginning to soften. You need them yielding but not collapsed — they go back into the oven with the filling, so undercooking slightly here is correct.

4. **Make the filling while the aubergines roast.** Heat 1 tbsp olive oil in a small frying pan over medium heat. Add the red onion and cook for 5 minutes until soft and translucent. Add the garlic and green chilli and cook for another 90 seconds. Add the cumin, coriander, smoked paprika, and black pepper. Stir for 30 seconds — you are blooming the spices in the oil, not just coating the onion with them. Remove from heat.

5. **Combine the filling.** In a bowl, mix the spiced onion mixture with the crushed walnuts, lemon juice, fresh coriander, and salt. Taste and adjust: it should be assertive, because it will share space with the creamy chevre.

6. **Mix the chevre.** In a small bowl, work the chevre with the olive oil, dried thyme, and black pepper until smooth and spreadable. Room temperature chevre is essential — cold chevre will tear the aubergine flesh when you press it in.

7. **Fill the aubergines.** Remove them from the oven. Gently press each aubergine open at the slits. Press a generous teaspoon of chevre into the base of each cavity first — this is the layer that melts into the flesh. Then press 1–2 tablespoons of the walnut filling on top of the chevre, packing it in firmly.

8. **Second roast.** Return the filled aubergines to the oven for 10–12 minutes until the chevre is visibly melted and the walnut filling has darkened slightly at the edges. The aubergine skins should be deep, wrinkled, and blistered in places. That blistering means the flesh inside is fully collapsed and smoky.

9. **Plate and finish.** Transfer to a serving plate. Scatter extra fresh coriander and a few walnut pieces over the top. If using pomegranate molasses, drizzle it now — about ½ tsp per aubergine is enough. Serve immediately.

## Variations

**Vegetable swap — courgette:** Halve small courgettes lengthways, scoop out a channel in the centre, and use the same filling. Roasting time in the first stage drops to 12 minutes. The result is lighter and less smoky, which suits summer.

**Cheaper cut — standard aubergine:** Halve one large aubergine lengthways, score the flesh in a crosshatch pattern, roast cut-side down for 20 minutes, then flip and fill as above. This is the weeknight version; it feeds two from one aubergine.

**Cheese substitution:** If chevre is unavailable, use labneh (strained yogurt) blended with 1 tsp lemon juice and ½ tsp dried mint. The flavour is more acidic and less rich, and it will not melt the same way — it stays in layers rather than integrating with the flesh. Honest difference, still a good dish.

**Adding heat:** Replace the smoked paprika with ½ tsp of Kashmiri chilli powder. This shifts the colour of the filling from rust-orange to deep red and increases the heat noticeably. It also makes the dish read more clearly Pakistani in character.

## Storage and Reheating

Store leftover stuffed aubergines in an airtight container in the refrigerator for up to 2 days. The filling firms up when cold. To reheat, place on a baking tray and warm in a 180°C / 350°F oven for 10 minutes — not a microwave, which makes the aubergine skin rubbery and steams the filling soggy. They are also edible at room temperature, which makes them reasonable mezze leftovers.

Do not freeze. The texture of roasted aubergine after freezing and thawing is unpleasant.

## FAQ

### What is goat cheese stuffed aubergine?
Goat cheese stuffed aubergine is a dish where baby aubergines are slit, partially roasted, filled with soft chevre and a spiced mixture, then returned to the oven until the cheese melts into the flesh and the filling caramelizes slightly at the edges.

### Can I use regular eggplant instead of baby aubergines for this stuffed baingan recipe?
Yes — halve a large aubergine lengthways, score the flesh, roast cut-side down for 20 minutes to soften, then flip and fill it. The technique is identical; only the presentation differs.

### Does the goat cheese melt inside the aubergine?
Soft fresh chevre at room temperature will melt into the hot aubergine flesh during the second roast, becoming creamy and integrated rather than sitting as a distinct layer. Aged or cold chevre will not behave the same way.

### What does pomegranate molasses do in this dish?
Pomegranate molasses adds tartness that cuts the richness of the chevre and a background sweetness that complements the smoked paprika. It is a finishing ingredient, not a cooking one — add it at the table, not during roasting.

### Is this chevre aubergine dish suitable for meal prep?
It can be prepped up to the filling stage (steps 1–6) several hours ahead and refrigerated. Complete the final roast just before serving. Fully cooked, it keeps for two days in the refrigerator and reheats well in the oven.

### Why score the aubergine in a cross pattern rather than just slitting it once?
Two perpendicular slits create four sections that fan open like petals when pressed, giving a larger cavity for filling and more surface area for the chevre to contact the hot flesh. A single slit gives a narrower pocket and the filling tends to fall out.

### Can I make walnut stuffed eggplant without chevre?
Yes — substitute labneh mixed with lemon juice and dried mint. The dish is tangier, slightly less rich, and the filling stays in more distinct layers rather than melting into the aubergine. It is a different dish, not a lesser one.
