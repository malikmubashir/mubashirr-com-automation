## Headnote

This recipe started with a problem: I had two beautiful rainbow trout from a fish farm in the Savoie, and no interest in cooking them the way every French recipe insists — meunière, beurre blanc, or buried under almonds. Trout is a delicate fish, but delicate does not mean it needs to be treated gently by flavour. What it cannot handle is aggressive heat or acid that breaks its texture before you've had a chance to eat it.

The solution is a coconut milk poaching liquor tempered with black mustard seeds and fresh curry leaves. The fat in coconut milk protects the flesh from overcooking, the mustard seeds give a bass note of bitterness, and the curry leaves do what curry leaves always do — make the whole thing smell like something worth sitting down for. The trout coconut curry that results is not Indian, not Pakistani, not French. It is what happens when you cook honestly from where you are.

## Ingredients

### For the trout
- 4 rainbow trout fillets, skin on, pin-boned, approximately 180 g (6 oz) each
- 1 tsp fine sea salt
- ½ tsp ground turmeric
- ½ tsp Kashmiri chilli powder

### For the coconut mustard gravy
- 2 tbsp neutral oil (sunflower or grapeseed)
- 1 tsp black mustard seeds
- 1 tsp cumin seeds
- 12–15 fresh curry leaves
- 1 medium white onion, finely diced (about 150 g / 5 oz)
- 4 garlic cloves, minced
- 1 tsp fresh ginger, grated fine (about a 2 cm / ¾ inch piece)
- 2 green chillies, slit lengthwise, seeds left in or out to taste
- 1 tsp ground coriander
- ½ tsp ground cumin
- ¼ tsp ground black pepper
- 400 ml (14 fl oz) full-fat coconut milk, from one standard tin
- 100 ml (3½ fl oz) fish stock or light chicken stock
- 1 tsp Dijon mustard
- 1 tsp fresh lime juice
- Salt to taste

### To finish
- Small handful of fresh coriander leaves
- Extra curry leaves, fried in a little oil until crisp (optional)
- Thinly sliced green chilli

## Ingredient Notes

**Black mustard seeds:** These are not the yellow mustard seeds used in American mustard. Black mustard seeds are smaller, rounder, and more pungent. They need to be bloomed in hot oil until they pop; unpopped seeds are bitter and harsh. Find them at any South Asian grocery, or online. Do not substitute yellow mustard seeds — the flavour profile shifts too far.

**Fresh curry leaves:** Dried curry leaves are not a substitute here. The aromatic compounds that make curry leaves useful dissipate almost entirely when dried. Fresh leaves freeze well: buy a large bunch, strip them from the stems, freeze flat on a tray, then bag. They go straight from freezer into the oil.

**Dijon mustard:** A French ingredient added not out of novelty but because its sharp, grain-free emulsification pulls the coconut milk sauce together and echoes the mustard seed theme without adding texture. One teaspoon is enough to notice without it tasting European.

**Trout fillets:** Farmed rainbow trout is widely available and sustainable. Wild brown trout works too, though the flesh is slightly denser and takes another two minutes to cook through. Salmon can be substituted but the fat content changes the sauce balance — see Variations.

**Coconut milk:** Use full-fat. Light coconut milk will not create a sauce with enough body to poach in without the protein seizing.

## Method

1. **Season the fish.** Pat the trout fillets dry with kitchen paper. Mix the salt, turmeric, and Kashmiri chilli powder, then dust the flesh side of each fillet evenly. Set aside at room temperature for 10 minutes while you build the sauce. Drying and seasoning the flesh side only keeps the skin from stewing in the marinade.

2. **Bloom the seeds.** Heat the oil in a wide, deep skillet or sauté pan (28 cm / 11 inch) over medium-high heat. When the oil shimmers, add the black mustard seeds and cumin seeds. Keep a lid nearby. Within 30–45 seconds the mustard seeds will begin to pop. Once they are mostly popped and the cumin is fragrant (about another 10–15 seconds), add the curry leaves immediately and stand back — they will spit aggressively from the water in the leaves.

3. **Build the base.** Add the onion to the pan and reduce heat to medium. Cook, stirring occasionally, until the onion is deep golden and softened at the edges, about 8–10 minutes. This is not a rushed step. Pale onion gives you a raw, sharp base; deep golden onion gives sweetness that balances the coconut fat.

4. **Add aromatics.** Add the garlic, ginger, and green chillies. Stir continuously for 90 seconds until the raw garlic smell cooks out. Add the ground coriander, ground cumin, and black pepper. Stir for another 30 seconds — you are toasting the ground spices in the residual fat, which intensifies them.

5. **Build the sauce.** Pour in the coconut milk and stock. Stir in the Dijon mustard. Increase heat to bring the liquid to a gentle simmer — not a boil. Taste and adjust salt. The sauce should be lightly seasoned at this point; the fish will season it further during poaching. Simmer uncovered for 4 minutes to let the sauce reduce slightly and come together.

6. **Poach the trout.** Reduce heat to low. Lay the trout fillets skin-side down into the sauce, spooning a little liquid over the top of each. Cover the pan with a lid and cook for 6–8 minutes. The fish is done when the flesh is opaque throughout and separates into flakes when pressed gently at the thickest point. Do not flip the fillets; poaching them on one side only keeps them intact.

7. **Finish and serve.** Remove from heat. Add the lime juice directly to the sauce around the fish, not on top of it — acid on cooked fish can grey the surface. Scatter fresh coriander leaves and sliced green chilli over the top. If using crisp-fried curry leaves, add them now so they hold their texture at the table.

## Variations

**Salmon instead of trout:** Use the same method, but salmon fillets at 180 g are thicker and fattier. Increase poaching time to 9–10 minutes and reduce the coconut milk by 50 ml (the extra fat from the fish enriches the sauce more than trout does). The flavour is deeper, the sauce slightly heavier.

**Whole spice-forward version (coconut mustard fish, drier style):** Reduce the stock to 50 ml and the coconut milk to 250 ml. After the fish is cooked, remove the fillets and reduce the sauce over high heat for 2–3 minutes until it coats a spoon. Return the fish briefly. This gives you something closer to a South Indian-style drier curry, which holds better in a lunchbox.

**Vegetarian version:** Substitute the trout with 400 g (14 oz) of firm tofu, pressed and cut into 3 cm cubes, or with 300 g of halved baby courgettes. Reduce poaching time to 4 minutes for tofu, 5 for courgettes. Use vegetable stock. The mustard-coconut-curry leaf combination works entirely without fish.

**Adding tomato:** One medium ripe tomato, grated, added after the spices in step 4 and cooked down for 3 minutes before adding the coconut milk, shifts the sauce into something tangier and slightly more Pakistani in profile. It also cuts some of the coconut sweetness, which can be welcome in summer.

## Storage and Reheating

Store leftover fish and sauce together in an airtight container in the refrigerator for up to 2 days. Do not freeze — the trout texture becomes waterlogged and the coconut milk splits on thawing.

To reheat: place in a covered skillet over the lowest heat you have, with 2 tablespoons of water added to prevent the sauce from catching. Heat for 5–6 minutes until barely steaming. Do not microwave fish if you can help it; the flesh overcooks in patches.

## FAQ

### Can I use dried curry leaves in this trout coconut curry?
Dried curry leaves will not work here — they lose almost all their aromatic compounds during drying and add nothing meaningful to the dish. Fresh curry leaves are sold in most South Asian grocery stores and freeze well for up to three months.

### Why do I season only the flesh side of the trout, not the skin?
Seasoning the skin draws out moisture and causes the skin to steam rather than hold together during poaching. Keeping the skin dry means it stays intact as a structural layer, making the fillet easier to lift and serve without breaking.

### What does the Dijon mustard do in a coconut curry?
Dijon mustard acts as a mild emulsifier and adds a clean, sharp note that prevents the coconut milk sauce from tasting flat or one-dimensionally sweet. It also ties thematically to the black mustard seeds without duplicating their texture.

### How do I know when the trout is done poaching?
Press the thickest part of the fillet gently with a finger or the back of a spoon. When the flesh separates into opaque flakes with light pressure, it is done. Translucent flesh in the centre means another minute or two with the lid on.

### Can I make the coconut mustard sauce ahead of time?
Yes. Make the sauce through step 5, cool, and refrigerate for up to 24 hours. When ready to serve, bring the sauce back to a gentle simmer and proceed with step 6. This actually improves the sauce — the curry leaves and mustard seeds steep further overnight.

### Is this dish spicy?
With the seeds left in two green chillies and ½ tsp of Kashmiri chilli, it is medium. Kashmiri chilli is primarily a colouring chilli with mild heat. For a hotter result, add a third chilli or use bird's eye. For a mild version, remove the seeds from the chillies and reduce to one.

### What should I serve with this?
Steamed basmati rice absorbs the coconut mustard sauce cleanly. Jeera rice also works well. Avoid bread that is too dense — naan competes with the sauce weight. A simple kachumber salad (onion, tomato, cucumber, lime) alongside cuts the coconut richness.
